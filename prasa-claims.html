PRASA Claims

If you were injured or lost a breadwinner in a train accident in South Africa, you may have a claim against the Passenger Rail Agency of South Africa (PRASA).

Most trains in South Africa are operated by PRASA. As a passenger on a train, PRASA has a duty of care towards you. As such, you may have a claim against them if they breached this duty of care.

As with claims against the Road Accident Fund, you will be able to claim damages for your injury, as well as medical expenses and loss of earnings if applicable. In the case of a deceased breadwinner, you will be able to claim loss of support and funeral expenses.

When claiming against PRASA with M.A.K. Ameen and Company you will not have to pay in any fees*. We operate using a contingency fee agreement. This means that if your claim against PRASA is not successful, you will not pay a cent. If it is successful our fees shall be 25% of the total claim.

Please note there are time limits in lodging claims! You must lodge your claim within 3 years from the date of your accident!

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

I was injured or assaulted on a train or platform — can I claim against PRASA?

Potentially, yes. PRASA, as the operator of South Africa's rail network, owes passengers a duty of care, and the courts have confirmed that failures such as poor security or faulty doors can found a valid claim for compensation.

Is there a strict deadline to claim against PRASA?

Yes — because PRASA is an organ of state, you generally need to give formal written notice of your intention to claim within six months of the incident, and specific procedural rules apply. Missing this window can sometimes still be fixed through a court application, but it's far safer to contact us as soon as possible after the incident.
