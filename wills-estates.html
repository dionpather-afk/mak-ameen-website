Wills & Deceased Estate Transfers

Whether you’re dead or alive, M.A.K. Ameen and Company is here to protect your interests.

If you’re concerned about your loved ones after you’ve shuffled off this mortal coil, then allow us to draft your will for you, so you can, quite literally, rest in peace. From creating a testamentary trust, to legacies, and other family heirlooms you may want to pass on, we can handle it all.

Furthermore, unlike your wedding vows, the services of M.A.K. Ameen and Company do not part after death. As you embark on your passage through the afterlife, we will ensure the safe passage of your assets from your estate to your loved ones. Frequently Asked Questions

How old do I need to be to make a valid will, and what makes it legally valid?

You need to be at least 16 years old. To be valid, a will must be in writing, signed by you on every page, and witnessed by two people over the age of 14 who sign it together with you. Getting these formalities right matters — a will that doesn't meet them can be challenged or set aside.

What happens if a loved one dies without a will?

Their estate is divided according to the Intestate Succession Act rather than their own wishes. A surviving spouse receives the greater of R250,000 or a child's share, with the remainder split among children; if there are no children, it passes to parents and then siblings. Importantly, an unmarried life partner has no automatic right to inherit without a valid will — one of the most common and painful surprises families face.
